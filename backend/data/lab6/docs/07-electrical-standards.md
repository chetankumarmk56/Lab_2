# Electrical Permit Standards

## When an Electrical Permit Is Required

An electrical permit is required for: new circuits, service panel changes or
upgrades, subpanels, EV charger installation, hot tub or pool wiring, and any
alteration of fixed wiring. Electrical work bundled into a building permit is
covered by that permit; standalone electrical work requires its own permit.

## Exempt Work (No Permit Needed)

The following may be done without a permit:

- Like-for-like replacement of switches, receptacles, and light fixtures on
  existing circuits rated 20 amps or less.
- Cord-and-plug connected appliances.
- Low-voltage systems under 50 volts, such as doorbells and thermostats.
- Temporary decorative lighting installed for fewer than 90 days.

Exempt work must still meet code; exemption from a permit is not exemption from
the electrical code.

## Who May Perform the Work

Electrical work requires a C-10 licensed electrical contractor. An
owner-builder may perform electrical work on their own primary residence under
the owner-builder exemption, except service panel work: panel changes,
upgrades, and main service equipment must always be performed by a C-10
licensed electrician, with no owner-builder exception.

## Inspection Stages

Electrical work is inspected in two stages: a rough-in inspection after wiring
is installed but before it is concealed by insulation or drywall, and a final
inspection with devices, fixtures, and panel labeling complete. Concealing
wiring before rough-in approval may result in an order to expose the work.

## Service Upgrades

Service upgrades above 200 amps require a load calculation prepared per the
adopted electrical code and submitted with the application. Utility disconnect
and reconnect must be coordinated by the contractor; the County does not
schedule utility work.
